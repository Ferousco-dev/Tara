### 2026-11-06: the attribution ladder catches up with the recency rule
**Implemented and tested offline. No captured signal tape and no real Meet run: this corrects a pin and its documentation to match behaviour the committed binder already had.**
- **The problem.** `channel_reuse` was pinned as damage: `tests/meet/test_attribution_eval.py` asserted that Sam's first frame is answered `"Alex"`, and `ATTRIBUTION_EVAL`, `SPEAKER_BINDING` and the 2026-10-24 entry below all described the previous owner's name surviving a 1 s gap. On a clean checkout that test fails (`assert None == 'Alex'`): the committed `binding.py` has `DEFAULT_RECENT_MS = 800` and `_recent()` refuses to claim a name whose tile has been dark longer than that, so the answer is `None` and the suite is red on this repository.
- **Root cause.** The recency requirement is in `binding.py` (`BinderConfig.recent_ms`; `None` restores the reference binder's behaviour) and is the rule that makes a reused channel safe, but no document mentioned it: `SPEAKER_BINDING`'s "what Tara does differently" table listed only two deviations from the reference, and the 2026-10-24 entry below still listed "a recency requirement on the winning name" as an unimplemented candidate fix. The fixture, its pin and the docs therefore describe the pre-rule binder.
- **Measured both ways, so the rule is the cause and not fixture drift.** Shipped defaults: `channel_reuse` 2/2 turns correct, 36/40 named, precision 1.000, leakage 0, `wrong=0`, `unknown=4`, `silent_named=14`, no repaints or dropouts. The same fixture with `BinderConfig(recent_ms=None)`: `wrong=5`, precision 0.844, leakage 0.156, `wrong_names=(('Sam', 'Alex'),)`, 1 repaint, 1 dropout — exactly the numbers the ladder used to pin. With the fixture's 2 s previous turn held fixed, a handover gap of 800 ms still carries the old name for 1 frame, 700 ms for 2, 400 ms for 5, 0–200 ms for 7, and 900 ms or more is silent rather than wrong.
- **The change.** `CHANNEL_REUSE` moves from `PINNED` to `SAFE` (13 fixtures unchanged: 10 in the zero-leak gate, 3 pinned). The stale pinned test is replaced by `test_a_reused_channel_is_released_once_the_previous_owner_goes_stale`, which asserts the measured result (an unknown first frame, no `"Alex"` label anywhere in Sam's turn, 4 unknown frames, precision 1.000), and by `test_the_reused_channel_leak_returns_when_the_recency_rule_is_disabled`, which keeps the old 5-frame number as a config-level regression so dropping or shortening the rule is visible twice over. No threshold changed and no assertion was dropped without a stronger one replacing it.
- **Docs.** `ATTRIBUTION_EVAL` gains the recency rule and the sub-window boundary in finding 1, moves `channel_reuse` into the honest-fixture block of the ladder table, and counts its cost as unknown frames; `SPEAKER_BINDING` gains the recency rule as the third deliberate deviation from the reference and rewrites its known limit to state what the window does and does not cover.
- **Checks run.** `tests/meet` 80 passed, 10 skipped (the real-Chrome tests skip without Chrome); full offline suite `772 passed, 2 failed, 28 skipped, 7 deselected` — both failures are the pre-existing `tests/legacy` barge-in timing race, which fails identically at `532e362` and is untouched here; `ruff check src tests` clean; `mypy src` clean (146 files).
- **Not done.** No real Meet run, no captured signal tape, no latency or audio-quality claim. The recency rule still has no dedicated golden case in `tests/meet/test_binding.py` (the ladder covers it at eval level), and handovers inside the 800 ms window still name the previous owner — recorded above rather than fixed, because making them safe changes `binding.py`'s contract.

### 2026-10-24: the attribution ladder — the binder is scored against known turns
**Implemented and tested offline. No captured signal tape and no real Meet run: this measures the binder's decision rules, not its behaviour on a real call.**
- **The problem.** The entry below chose its thresholds against hand-written golden cases. A naming change judged on the case that motivated it has been judged against the one sample guaranteed to agree with it, and the two errors are not symmetric: UNKNOWN costs a label, while a wrong name is a false statement the engine may act on.
- **The ladder.** `src/tara/meet/eval.py` replays a `Scenario` — true turns plus the glow edges Meet actually showed — through the real `ChannelBinder` on a virtual clock, and scores every frame: completeness (a true turn named at all), leakage (a named frame that is not the person talking; the hard gate), attribution precision with the unknown rate beside it, plus repaints, dropouts and the silent decay tail. `tests/meet/attribution_fixtures.py` is 13 committed fixtures in ladder order; `tests/meet/test_attribution_eval.py` holds the gate, the per-fixture assertions, the pinned damage and the scorer's own units. Determinism is asserted: the same fixture replays to byte-identical frames.
- **Result.** Every honest fixture — truthful, 150/300 ms late or flickering glow, overlap, duplicate stream, reconnect, muted channel — has **zero wrong names**. The cost is unknown frames (overlap 15/70, 700 ms turns 11/42, 300 ms glow lag 5/30, a 100 ms flicker 0). Four fixtures pin damage where the premise breaks: a late self name (4 wrong frames, then purged), a channel reused by a different person (5 wrong frames: the previous owner's name survives a 1 s gap), a stuck glow (20 wrong frames, the whole other turn) and a glow that misses one of two simultaneous voices (28 wrong frames, 100 % leakage on the first-fed channel).
- **Finding, not fixed.** A reused channel keeps the previous owner for seconds because evidence is a decayed count with no cap; Vexa's binder has the same shape. Candidate fixes (a saturating score cap, a recency requirement on the winning name, or counting loud-without-that-name frames as negative evidence) change `binding.py`'s contract, so they are recorded in the doc for the owner rather than smuggled into an eval change. *(Correction 2026-11-06: the recency requirement is already implemented as `BinderConfig.recent_ms`; the numbers above are what the fixture produces with it disabled. See the entry at the top.)*
- **Docs.** New [docs/google-meet/ATTRIBUTION_EVAL.md](docs/google-meet/ATTRIBUTION_EVAL.md) (metrics, replay rules, the ladder table, findings, what is not verified, the Vexa derivation); `SPEAKER_BINDING.md` gains "How it is scored" and "Known limits (measured)"; `docs/google-meet/README.md` links it.
- **Checks run.** `tests/meet/test_attribution_eval.py` 19 passed; `tests/meet` 89 passed (real-Chrome tests included); full offline suite `783 passed, 18 skipped, 7 deselected`; `ruff check src tests` clean; `mypy src` clean (146 files).
- **Not done.** No real signal tape, no real Meet run and no latency/cost claim; the 100 ms binary-energy frame model is a simplification; the reused-channel leak above remains open.
- **Correction (2026-11-06).** The reused-channel finding in this entry, the ladder table and `SPEAKER_BINDING` described the leak as current, but `binding.py` in this commit already requires a winning name's tile to be lit or dark for at most `recent_ms` (800 ms). The 5 wrong frames are what the same fixture produces with that rule disabled; with the shipped defaults it is 0 wrong and 4 unknown. Re-measured, corrected and pinned in the 2026-11-06 entry above.

### 2026-10-24: who said what in Meet — per-participant channels named by energy ↔ glow
**Implemented and tested offline and in a real Chrome against the local stand-in for Meet. Not verified on the real Google Meet page; no latency, cost or audio-quality claim.**
- **The problem.** Meet has no structural audio ↔ participant link: every remote participant has their own `MediaStream`, but the elements are an anonymous rotating pool and the "who is speaking" glow is global. Naming a chunk from whoever is lit at that instant leaks a name across channels during overlap, and a wrong name is worse than none because identity-gated rules act on it.
- **The port.** `src/tara/meet/binding.py` correlates a channel's audio energy with Meet's glow over a sliding window (`tau_ms` 2500, loud 0.02, floor 2.5): a channel belongs to the tile that is lit exactly while that channel is loud. Decay, a confidence floor and one-tile-one-channel keep it honest; below the floor, in a near-tie or on an exact tie, the channel stays unknown. The algorithm and the page-side technique are derived from Vexa (`gmeet-channel-binder.ts`, `gmeet-capture.ts`, `pcm-capture.ts`, `gmeet-speakers.ts`; Apache-2.0, read 2026-10-23). No Vexa code, service, runtime or new dependency is used.
- **Stricter than the reference, deliberately.** (a) A best name must lead the runner-up by `margin` (1.5×) or nobody is named; (b) an exact tie between two channels for one tile claims nobody (the reference lets the first caller win). Both are documented in [docs/google-meet/SPEAKER_BINDING.md](docs/google-meet/SPEAKER_BINDING.md).
- **Page side.** `meet/bridge.js` gains an opt-in per-participant mode: one 16 kHz `AudioContext` per remote media element with an `AudioWorklet` (ScriptProcessor fallback) and a silent sink, plus a glow scanner that reports speaking onsets/offsets and the local tile's name. The mixed tap keeps working until the first channel frame arrives, then stops for good in the page, so nobody is heard twice and a page that never delivers channels keeps the old behaviour. `meet/bridge.py` exposes the channel/glow/self callbacks, counters and `AudioBridge.bind()` diagnostics.
- **Adapter and engine.** `meet/adapter.py` stamps a bound channel with the participant id the engine already knows (`a` for the candidate, `p-<name>` otherwise) and leaves an unbound one as `ch-<n>` (unknown); it resets the binder on reconnect, pins the bot's own tile and reports channels in the run log. `voice/engine.py` now learns participant ids from join/leave/rename so a bound stream id resolves to a name (previously only the startup roster was known). `meet/run.py` adds `--per-participant`, off by default and refused with `--audio devices` (the taps only exist on the page route).
- **Recording, honestly.** `meet/recorder.py` writes one WAV per channel under `--record`, because several people at once cannot share one sequential timeline; the mixed `room` track is unchanged on the default path. With `--record`, the AI-presence line the meeting is told no longer says "Nothing is recorded as audio or video".
- **Checks run.** `tests/meet/test_binding.py` 13 passed (new golden cases); `tests/meet/test_meet_unit.py` adds adapter-level cases (unbound `ch-<n>`, earned name, the bot's own tile never bound, reconnect reset, one recording file per channel) and a CLI refusal for `--per-participant` with `--audio devices`, and passes; `tests/meet/test_meet_browser.py` 10 passed in a real Chrome (including the new two-speaker test: two channels, two names, mixed tap stopped, the bot's own preview never a channel); full offline suite `764 passed, 18 skipped, 7 deselected`; `ruff check src tests` clean; `mypy src` clean (145 files). One later re-run of the whole `tests/meet` folder hit the documented pre-existing real-Chrome audio flake (`peakRms` just under 0.2 in `test_audio_flows_both_ways_through_real_chrome`); it passed on its own, and the code path it exercises is unchanged by this work.
- **Not done.** No real Meet run of this mode, so the tile selectors, `span.notranslate`, `data-self-name` and the speaking class names remain unverified, and the page audio route itself is unproven on real Meet (the real runs used virtual cables; on the page route the WebRTC tap heard silence in four runs), which per-participant capture inherits; no measurement of cost, latency or echo with one `AudioContext` per participant; per-turn speaker labels do not yet reach interview reports or minutes. `voice/engine.py` changed, which the repo rules put in Claude's review lane.

### 2026-10-23: memory that survives the process, and retrieval that can explain itself
**Implemented and tested offline and against a real local PostgreSQL; live Gemini behaviour is still unverified. Verified: `718 passed, 10 skipped, 7 deselected`; `ruff check src tests` clean; `mypy src` clean (140 files); real-Postgres memory suite `10 passed` in a throwaway schema. These are suite results, not voice-latency measurements.**
- **What was wrong.** An audit found that SESSION-tier notes (decisions, actions, questions) existed only in `voice.memory.InMemoryMeetingMemory` and vanished on exit; the durable store used `action` while live notes and tools said `action_item`; a correction became a disconnected row unless the caller supplied `supersedes_id`; retrieval was full-text search plus recency with no kind/time/status handling; a proposal ("we should probably use PostgreSQL") could be extracted as a `decision`; and a restart could not tell a finished meeting from an abandoned one. ADR-0016 records the decisions.
- **One vocabulary.** New `src/tara/memory/kinds.py` owns canonical persisted kinds and the explicit translations (`action_item -> action`, `open_question`/`proposal -> note`); `MemoryItem` normalizes legacy spellings, and tools and minutes translate once instead of comparing across vocabularies.
- **Proposals are not decisions.** New `src/tara/memory/claims.py` classifies assertion, preference, decision, proposal, action, question, hypothetical and correction. A proposal or hypothetical is stored as a tentative `note` candidate; only committed wording ("we have decided") can produce a decision. A repeated proposal still does not become agreement. Minutes now have a separate "Proposals (not agreed)" section.
- **Session notes are durable.** `AutoMemory` writes the live note and a candidate record in the same turn task, so an ordinary meeting's decisions and actions survive the process without anyone saying "remember this".
- **Corrections link, or stay flagged.** A `correction` resolves its target by token overlap plus full-text search. A clear winner is superseded only after the new row is written (old row keeps `status=superseded`, `superseded_by`, source and timestamps). Two nearly equal candidates supersede nothing and set `possible_conflict` and `correction_candidates`; the model is told to report both. A model-proposed `corrects` reference is resolved against rows Tara actually holds and ignored otherwise.
- **Conservative consolidation.** `MemoryService.consolidate` merges only same-kind records with token Jaccard >= 0.9, numbers significant (so "Decision 1: ship" and "Decision 2: ship" are never merged), bounded to the newest 200, and records `duplicate_of`. An exact repeated candidate is reinforced (`sightings`), not duplicated. It runs once per start in a background task.
- **Retrieval with filters and a re-ranker.** New `src/tara/memory/retrieval.py` parses "today / yesterday / last week / last N days" and kind hints ("what did we decide") and applies them in the store before ranking; `rank_hits` adds rarity-weighted keyword coverage, phrase match, confirmation status, kind, a 45-day recency half-life, conflict penalty and a near-duplicate diversity pass. A filter with no keyword match returns the newest items in the window, labelled `recent`, rather than nothing. `recall_memory` reports period, meeting number/date, labels and conflict warnings, and says plainly when it has nothing.
- **Startup context is ordered.** One summary per meeting, then corrections, decisions, open action items, other confirmed items, candidates, then at most three transcript chunks; meeting number and date are included, candidates are labelled unconfirmed, tentative proposals "proposal, not agreed". `render_memories` counts its header against the character budget and truncates instead of overrunning.
- **Restart recovery is explicit.** Migration `005_recovery_and_ranking.sql` adds `meeting_sessions.interrupted`, `owner_pid`, `owner_host` and retrieval indexes. At start, sessions left open by a dead process are closed at their last activity and marked interrupted ("recovered after a restart"); a session owned by another live process on the same host is left alone. A normal exit still marks the meeting completed.
- **Also:** `MemoryService.recover`, `meeting_brief` now names the current meeting and lists earlier ones with what they left open; `voice/app.py` runs recovery and background consolidation at start and cancels them on close; `dev.sh` applies memory migrations before local, interview, and Meet voice sessions; migrations and the ADR index updated; `tests/memory/test_memory_retrieval.py` added (20 offline tests) and `tests/memory/test_memory_postgres.py` extended with filtered retrieval, implicit correction, recovery and a two-store restart plus correction.
- **Not done, deliberately.** No embeddings/pgvector, no Redis, no document RAG (there is still no ingestion, chunk store, document ACL or deletion path), no per-document ACL/RLS. Retrieval quality and latency have not been measured with memory enabled, and the real Gemini model calling these tools is unverified. Docs: [docs/memory/MEMORY_OPERATIONS.md](docs/memory/MEMORY_OPERATIONS.md), [docs/memory/MEMORY_AND_RAG.md](docs/memory/MEMORY_AND_RAG.md), [docs/adr/0016-memory-retrieval-consolidation-recovery.md](docs/adr/0016-memory-retrieval-consolidation-recovery.md).

### Interview answers are judged by meaning, with a real retry and a real three-minute limit (offline-tested)
**Implemented and tested offline: `608 passed, 6 skipped, 7 deselected`; `ruff check src tests` and `mypy src` clean. No live Gemini or spoken interview was run.**
- **Wrong scoring path replaced.** The old live path asked the model for a 0–4 score and an exact quote, so a correct answer in different words could be rejected and a confident wrong answer could look strong. `src/tara/hiring/evaluator.py` now asks the configured text model to judge technical accuracy, understanding, completeness, reasoning, examples and trade-offs, accepts a valid alternative approach, and returns validated `correctness`, `technical_accuracy`, `completeness`, `reasoning`, `feedback`, `hint` and `missing_concepts`. Code computes a 0–10 weighted score (45% technical accuracy, 25% completeness, 30% reasoning) and caps incorrect/unclear and partial answers; a `correct`/`mostly_correct`/`valid_alternative` label with technical accuracy or completeness below 5/10 is downgraded to `partially_correct`, and only correct-ish labels can pass. Confidence alone cannot raise the result. The existing report scale remains 0–4 via a documented mapping.
- **One retry, preserved attempts.** `src/tara/hiring/interview.py` now has `InterviewState`, checked `VALID_TRANSITIONS`, per-question `QuestionProgress`, attempt contexts and stale-result rejection. An incorrect or unclear first answer gets one retry with a short hint and does not advance the question; a second failure advances without revealing the answer. Every attempt is kept in `question_results`; only the final attempt feeds criterion averages, so a retry never creates an extra scored question.
- **Timer and turn-taking.** Each question gets a monotonic fallback deadline in code. When Tara's first completed audio playback after the question issue finishes, the clock starts from that point via the existing `PlaybackController.on_finished` signal; a muted, interrupted or failed path keeps the tool-return fallback. The start is idempotent, a retry shares the deadline, a late result cannot change the active question, and a stale timeout decision cannot cancel the next question's timer. At three minutes the application records a timeout, stops accepting content for that question, does not evaluate an incomplete answer, and prompts the model to move on once. An answer completed before the deadline may finish evaluating after it. The response-level signal cannot tell a preamble from the question wording, so a separate preamble response can start the clock a few seconds early; this is documented. The engine now ignores duplicate final input transcripts, and `WorkServices` captures the live answer before `TurnComplete`, waits briefly for delayed transcription, and rejects duplicate tool calls. `LiveTaraApp` no longer pauses one second before the opening, sends the welcome once, and uses a 1200 ms interview silence window unless `TARA_LIVE_SILENCE_MS` is set.
- **Settings and tests.** `TARA_INTERVIEW_QUESTION_LIMIT_S` (180), `TARA_INTERVIEW_RETRY_LIMIT` (1), `TARA_INTERVIEW_EVAL_TIMEOUT_S` (20) and `TARA_INTERVIEW_ANSWER_GRACE_S` (2) are parsed and validated.
